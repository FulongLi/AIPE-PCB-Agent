MISSION

Act as a Principal Power Electronics and Hardware
Design Engineer with expertise in:

- Power electronics converter design
- SiC MOSFET and gate-driver engineering
- High-frequency switching circuits
- Advanced PCB placement and routing
- Electromagnetic compatibility (EMI/EMC)
- Analogue and mixed-signal electronics
- High-speed digital PCB design
- DSP-based digital control
- Power integrity and signal integrity
- Thermal and mechanical engineering
- Electrical insulation and safety
- PCB manufacturing and assembly
- Circuit modelling and simulation

Your responsibility is to independently redesign
the complete 5 kW SiC converter from first principles.

This is NOT another incremental patch to V2.2.

Create a NEW V3 engineering design using the
requirements, validated knowledge and lessons
from previous generations.

Reconsider the system architecture, circuitry,
component placement and PCB implementation.

Carry the engineering work through to real,
editable KiCad projects and verified outputs.

The goal is a professional, knowledge-driven
engineering prototype design.

This is one comprehensive autonomous task.

Do not split the implementation into multiple
independent user-requested tasks.

Use internal engineering milestones and
verification gates, but continue working
without routine approval requests.

Ask for user input only when a genuinely
unresolvable requirement or safety-critical
decision prevents responsible progress.

3. FUNDAMENTAL DESIGN REQUIREMENTS
==================================================

Design a complete synchronous Buck converter.

Nominal input voltage:
400 V DC

Input operating range:
360–440 V DC

Nominal output voltage:
200 V DC

Rated output power:
5000 W

Rated output current:
25 A

Nominal switching frequency:
50 kHz

Power semiconductor technology:
Wolfspeed SiC MOSFET

Digital controller:
TI TMS320F28335

PCB design environment:
KiCad

Preferred PCB layer count:
2–4 layers where technically feasible.

Six layers may be used when justified.

Power/control partitioning:
To be decided independently.

The following parameters must be defined
and justified during system design:

- Efficiency target
- Output voltage regulation
- Output voltage ripple
- Inductor current ripple
- Ambient temperature
- Cooling arrangement
- Maximum permissible temperatures
- Transient overload requirements
- Startup requirements
- Fault response requirements
- PCB material and stack-up
- Electrical insulation environment
- Mechanical envelope
- PCB manufacturing capabilities

Clearly distinguish:

USER REQUIREMENTS

VERIFIED DATASHEET VALUES

ANALYTICAL DESIGN RESULTS

ENGINEERING ASSUMPTIONS

UNVERIFIED DESIGN TARGETS

Do not silently convert assumptions
into established requirements.

    ↓

- Current sensing
- Temperature sensing
- Fast hardware protection
- DSP controller
- Communication interfaces
- Board-to-board interfaces
- Mechanical and thermal systems

Evaluate alternative partitioning options:

A. Single integrated PCB

B. Separate power and control PCBs

C. Separate power, gate-driver and
   digital-control assemblies

Select the architecture using engineering
criteria rather than appearance alone.

Provide a documented architecture
comparison and selection.


==================================================
5. INDEPENDENT COMPONENT SELECTION
==================================================

Review the major component choices.

Do not automatically retain V2.2 components.

Select suitable real manufacturer devices.

POWER MOSFETS

Evaluate Wolfspeed SiC MOSFETs based on:

- VDS rating
- Transient overvoltage margin
- Continuous and peak current
- RDS(on) over temperature
- Gate charge
- Eon / Eoff
- Coss
- Package parasitics
- Kelvin-source availability
- Junction-to-case thermal resistance
- Mounting arrangement
- Datasheet operating limits

The existing C4MS025120K is a candidate,
not an unquestionable requirement.

GATE DRIVERS

Evaluate:

- Isolation rating
- Output drive capability
- Compatible gate bias
- Miller clamp
- DESAT / short-circuit protection
- UVLO
- Fault reporting
- Propagation delay
- Common-mode transient immunity
- Local supply decoupling

The existing UCC21710-Q1 may be retained
if justified.

CAPACITORS

Evaluate:

- DC-link capacitance
- RMS ripple current
- ESR and ESL
- Voltage derating
- Film/ceramic combinations
- Temperature and lifetime
- Actual package and mounting geometry

MAGNETIC COMPONENTS

Evaluate:

- Required output inductance
- Actual inductance under DC bias
- Peak current
- Saturation behaviour
- Copper losses
- Core losses
- Winding temperature
- Stray-field effects
- Mechanical dimensions
- Cooling requirements

SENSORS

Evaluate current and voltage sensors
against the actual operating conditions.

Do not repeat the V2.1 TMCS1133
reference-law error.

Consider required bidirectional current
capability, offset, bandwidth, noise,
temperature and ADC interface.

6. COMPLETE ELECTRICAL CIRCUIT DESIGN
==================================================

Design the electrical circuit before PCB routing.

Include:

- Complete power stage
- Gate-driver circuits
- Isolated gate-bias supplies
- Input protection
- DC-link
- Output filter
- Voltage/current sensing
- Hardware overcurrent protection
- Overvoltage protection
- Undervoltage protection
- Thermal protection
- Fault interlocks
- PWM disable and fail-safe behaviour
- Auxiliary supplies
- DSP power and clock systems
- ADC conditioning
- Communication and debug interfaces

Verify compatibility between all circuit
elements.

For example:

The selected current sensor must be
compatible with its reference scheme,
power supply, signal-conditioning circuit,
ADC input range and firmware conversion.

The isolated gate-bias supply must meet
actual load and dynamic-current requirements.

Dead time must not be assumed to exist
merely because complementary PWM signals
have interlock logic.

A clean ERC is not sufficient proof
of electrical correctness.

7. SOLVE THE PREVIOUS ENGINEERING ISSUES
==================================================

V2.2 identified 18 engineering issues.

Use the complete E22-01 through E22-18
register as a mandatory V3 design input.

Create:

review/v22_issue_resolution_matrix.md

For every previous issue provide:

- Original issue ID
- Root cause
- V3 design response
- Modified circuitry or geometry
- Supporting analysis
- Verification method
- Closure status
- Remaining risk

Pay particular attention to:

A. UCC14141 VEEA feedback return

Design a physically quiet feedback-star
network with correct return-current paths.

Do not declare compliance based only on
net names or schematic connectivity.

B. SiC commutation loop

Reconsider high-side MOSFET, low-side
MOSFET and HF DC-link capacitor placement.

Minimise the complete high-di/dt loop,
including return copper and layer transitions.

C. Low-side gate loop

Reconsider driver position, gate resistors,
Miller clamp and Kelvin-source routing.

D. High-current copper bottlenecks

Review 25 A continuous current paths,
sensor package transitions, vias,
copper thickness and thermal limits.

E. Electrical insulation

Resolve clearance/creepage requirements
using the intended working voltage,
environment and applicable standards.

Do not accept unexplained DRC exceptions.

F. Mechanical assembly

Resolve control-board mounting,
connector positioning, heatsink interfaces,
device mounting and mechanical support.

G. Protection architecture

Resolve precharge, inrush, relay,
fuse/MOV coordination, discharge
and fault-shutdown requirements.

H. EMI and thermal risks

Address them as design constraints,
not as a final visual inspection.

Do not report an issue as fully closed
unless the required evidence exists.

Some items require hardware measurements.
Mark those as:

DESIGN ADDRESSED — HARDWARE VALIDATION PENDING

rather than claiming experimental closure.

==================================================

- Path simplification
- 45-degree routing
- Bend minimisation
- Tangent arc transitions
- Routing quality checks

ELECTRICAL QUALITY FIRST.

Explicitly consider:

- Complete high-di/dt current loops
- Gate and Kelvin-return routing
- Power-plane continuity
- Controlled return-current paths
- Critical analogue routes
- High-current copper
- Layer transitions
- Via arrays
- Noise coupling
- Isolation barriers
- Thermal behaviour

Prefer clean and organised routing.

Avoid:

- Excessive right-angle bends
- Unnecessary zigzags
- Short repetitive jogs
- Unnecessary vias

or reduce copper current capacity
merely to improve appearance.

on actual engineering requirements.

Prioritise four-layer implementations
where feasible, but justify six layers
if they offer a meaningful advantage.
