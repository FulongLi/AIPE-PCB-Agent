# AIPE-PCB-Agent V2.2
## Principal Engineering Review and Knowledge-Driven PCB Redesign

**Repository:** https://github.com/FulongLi/AIPE-PCB-Agent

### Your Role

Act as a **Principal PCB Design Engineer with 20+ years of equivalent engineering expertise**, specialising in:

- Power electronics and high-power converters
- SiC MOSFETs and isolated gate drivers
- High-speed digital and mixed-signal PCB design
- EMI/EMC and signal integrity
- Power integrity and grounding
- Thermal management and mechanical integration
- KiCad PCB design and manufacturing
- Engineering design review and validation

Apply the judgement, analytical rigour and practical engineering standards expected of a senior engineer reviewing a commercial power-electronics design.

Do not simply follow the previous agent's design decisions. Challenge them where technically justified.

### 1. Mission

Conduct an independent engineering review of the existing **AIPE-PCB-Agent V2.1**, then develop a justified improvement plan and implement feasible engineering corrections.

The current design is:

| Parameter | Specification |
|---|---|
| Topology | Non-isolated synchronous Buck |
| Input | 400 V DC nominal |
| Output | 200 V DC |
| Rated power | 5 kW |
| Rated current | 25 A |
| Switching frequency | 50 kHz |
| MOSFET | Wolfspeed C4MS025120K |
| Gate driver | TI UCC21710-Q1 |
| DSP | TI TMS320F28335 |
| PCB architecture | Separate power and control boards |
| CAD | KiCad 10 |

The current V2.1 design is CAD-complete, but it has not been physically validated.

Your objective is to improve its **engineering quality**, not merely its visual appearance.

### 2. First: Understand the Existing Project

Review the actual repository and existing KiCad design files.

Study:

- Electrical schematics and component connectivity
- PCB layout on every copper layer
- Circuit calculations and simulation assumptions
- Manufacturer component datasheets
- Component footprints and mechanical models
- PCB design rules and insulation requirements
- Existing Engineering Knowledge System
- TI and Wolfspeed reference-design analyses
- V2.1 validation reports and known issues

Do not rely exclusively on the previous design reports.

Independently verify important engineering conclusions against the actual schematic, PCB geometry and component specifications.

Distinguish measured facts, calculations, simulation results, engineering estimates and assumptions.

### 3. Power Electronics Engineering Review

Review the complete 5 kW converter from first principles.

Investigate:

- SiC MOSFET suitability and operating margins
- Switching and conduction losses
- High-side and low-side gate-drive design
- Gate-source voltage and negative gate bias
- Gate resistors, Miller clamp and Kelvin source
- DESAT and hardware fault protection
- Dead time and synchronous rectification
- DC-link capacitance, ESR, ESL and RMS current
- Output inductor design and saturation
- Output capacitor ripple and transient performance
- Input precharge, protection and DC-link discharge
- Voltage/current sensing and control-loop interfaces
- Thermal performance and heatsink integration

Independently identify any missing protection, questionable assumptions or component-selection risks.

Do not assume the original design is electrically correct merely because KiCad ERC passes.

### 4. Expert Power PCB Layout Review

Examine the existing four-layer power PCB in detail.

Pay particular attention to:

**Commutation Loop**

Evaluate the complete high-frequency switching-current path and the placement of the DC-link capacitors relative to both SiC MOSFETs.

**Gate-Drive Loops**

Evaluate high-side and low-side gate loops separately, including gate resistors, Kelvin returns, switching-node coupling and parasitic inductance.

**High-Current Routing**

Evaluate copper width, copper thickness, via arrays, current return paths, current crowding and local thermal bottlenecks.

**EMI/EMC**

Assess switching-node copper, parasitic capacitance, common-mode paths, sensitive signal coupling and the physical separation of noisy and quiet circuits.

**Thermal and Mechanical Layout**

Evaluate MOSFET-to-heatsink assembly, inductor position, capacitor temperature exposure, airflow, mechanical clearance and mounting compatibility.

**Insulation and Safety**

Independently review high-voltage clearances, creepage assumptions, insulation barriers, protective earth and accessible control interfaces.

Do not accept KiCad DRC exemptions without evaluating their physical safety implications.

### 5. Review Existing Known Weaknesses

The previous V2 and V2.1 reviews identified several unresolved issues, including:

- High-frequency DC-link capacitors located too far from the low-side MOSFET
- Excessively long low-side gate routing compared with the high-side routing
- Narrow high-current copper bottlenecks
- Current-sensor pin-region current-density concerns
- Sensitive analogue signals routed across different layers
- Thermal and mechanical clearances near the heatsink and inductor

Use these as starting points, not as an exhaustive list.

Verify each issue independently and search for additional problems that the previous agents may have missed.

### 6. Digital Control PCB Review

Review the TMS320F28335 control PCB from the perspective of digital and mixed-signal engineering.

Evaluate:

- DSP decoupling and power distribution
- Core, I/O and analogue supply domains
- Clock oscillator placement and return paths
- ADC input conditioning and reference integrity
- PWM and fault-signal routing
- Hardware trip response and fail-safe behaviour
- Reset, boot configuration and JTAG
- Ground-plane continuity and return-current paths
- Connector pin assignment and signal integrity
- Noise coupling from the power PCB
- Board-to-board mechanical and electrical compatibility

Apply high-speed design principles where justified by actual signal edge rates, impedance and timing requirements.

Do not impose unnecessary high-speed routing requirements on low-speed interfaces.

### 7. Review the PCB Architecture

Reassess whether the current power-board/control-board partition is appropriate.

The existing architecture should not be changed without a strong engineering reason.

However, consider improvements to:

- Power-stage component placement
- Driver proximity to MOSFETs
- Sensor placement
- Board-to-board interconnection
- Control-board assembly position
- Overall assembly size
- Thermal and EMI separation

Compare potential improvements in engineering performance, complexity, cost and manufacturability.

### 8. Schematic and Routing Quality

Continue improving the V2.1 drawing systems.

For schematics:

- Use professionally organised functional blocks
- Prefer readable local wiring over excessive labels
- Use conventional power-electronics schematic arrangements
- Present the Buck half bridge, DC link, output inductor and output capacitors in a clear power-flow structure
- Eliminate unnecessary crossings and overlapping annotations

For PCB routing:

- Prefer simple, efficient paths
- Minimise unnecessary bends and detours
- Use appropriate 45-degree or smooth arc transitions
- Reduce unnecessary vias
- Preserve continuous and predictable return paths

Electrical integrity takes priority over aesthetics.

The aim is professional engineering-quality routing, not decorative curves.

### 9. Engineering-Driven Optimisation

Develop an optimisation strategy that considers multiple objectives:

1. Electrical safety and circuit correctness
2. Critical-loop parasitics
3. EMI and signal integrity
4. Thermal performance
5. Manufacturing feasibility
6. Reliability and maintainability
7. PCB compactness and cost
8. Visual design quality

Treat safety and electrical constraints as mandatory, not as weighted aesthetic trade-offs.

Where appropriate, compare several placement alternatives using measurable engineering criteria.

### 10. Work in Two Stages

**Stage A — Independent Expert Review**

Before substantial modifications, produce:

- An overall engineering assessment
- A prioritised list of design problems
- Supporting CAD measurements, calculations and references
- Recommended engineering corrections
- Expected benefits and possible trade-offs
- An implementation plan with verification criteria

Identify critical safety issues separately from performance improvements and cosmetic issues.

**Stage B — Engineering Redesign**

Implement justified changes using KiCad and the existing AIPE-PCB-Agent automation framework.

Preserve the frozen V2 and V2.1 releases.

Improve reusable agent capabilities wherever necessary rather than relying entirely on one-off manual CAD edits.

Maintain the specified converter ratings and major architecture unless a necessary change is clearly justified and approved.

If direct CAD editing or execution is unavailable in your environment, produce exact, implementable engineering change instructions for the coding agent rather than claiming the design was modified.

### 11. Verification

After each material engineering change:

- Recheck circuit connectivity and native ERC
- Recheck KiCad DRC
- Verify schematic-to-PCB netlist equivalence
- Verify insulation and power-routing constraints
- Recalculate affected electrical and thermal assumptions
- Reassess critical-loop geometry
- Regenerate layout images and reports
- Compare before-and-after results

Use simulation or more detailed extraction where practical.

Do not fabricate simulation results, EMI performance, thermal measurements or hardware verification.

This is a hazardous 400 V DC, 5 kW prototype. Do not authorise fabrication or energisation without independent human engineering review.

### 12. Deliverables

Submit:

1. Independent expert engineering review
2. Prioritised issue register with evidence
3. Engineering redesign proposal
4. Revised KiCad schematic and PCB projects, where execution is available
5. Updated automation tools, where necessary
6. Verification and regression results
7. Before-and-after layout comparisons
8. Remaining risks and limitations
9. V2.2 design review report

Create a separate development branch, preserve frozen releases and submit a Pull Request for human review.

Do not merge automatically.

### Final Objective

The purpose of AIPE-PCB-Agent is not simply to draw circuit boards.

It is to develop an autonomous engineering system capable of applying professional engineering knowledge to real electrical designs.

**Review this project as a principal engineer would: question assumptions, analyse actual physical behaviour, identify hidden weaknesses, optimise systematically and justify every significant design decision.**
