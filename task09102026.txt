# AIPE-PCB-Agent V2.0
# Knowledge-Driven Autonomous SiC Power Converter Design

Repository:
https://github.com/FulongLi/AIPE-PCB-Agent

Mission:
Design a complete 400 V to 200 V, 5 kW SiC synchronous
buck converter using the existing AIPE Engineering
Knowledge System and KiCad automation tools.

This is the second major design experiment for
AIPE-PCB-Agent.

V1 was produced using Codex.
V2 will be independently developed using Claude Code.

The objective is to evaluate whether engineering
knowledge extracted from real manufacturer reference
designs can improve autonomous electronic design.

Work autonomously through the complete engineering
design workflow.

Do not stop after calculations or schematic generation.

Produce complete, reviewable KiCad PCB projects.

==================================================
6. POWER SEMICONDUCTOR SELECTION
==================================================

Use Wolfspeed SiC MOSFETs.

Evaluate suitable candidates from the
650 V, 900 V and 1200 V classes.

Do not automatically choose the lowest
voltage rating.

Evaluate:

- voltage margin
- transient overshoot
- current ratings
- RDS(on)
- switching energy
- gate charge
- parasitic capacitances
- package
- Kelvin source
- thermal resistance
- availability

Select a real manufacturer part number.

Use verified datasheet parameters.

Estimate switching and conduction losses
at the proposed operating conditions.

Ensure the selected semiconductor can operate
within its applicable safe operating limits.


==================================================
7. GATE DRIVER DESIGN
==================================================

Apply Wolfspeed and TI gate-driver knowledge.

Design a complete SiC Gate Driver stage.

Evaluate:

- isolated gate driver IC
- positive and negative gate bias
- isolated bias supply
- gate resistor selection
- separate turn-on and turn-off paths
- Miller clamp
- Kelvin source connection
- DESAT or equivalent fast protection
- UVLO
- hardware interlock
- dead time
- fault signalling
- gate supply decoupling

Select real driver components.

Document why they are appropriate for
the selected SiC MOSFET.

The driver PCB layout must minimise
gate-loop parasitic inductance.

Do not blindly transplant a Wolfspeed
reference circuit with different operating
voltage, package or switching conditions.


==================================================
8. MAGNETIC COMPONENT DESIGN
==================================================

Design the output inductor.

Determine:

- required inductance
- rated current
- peak current
- saturation current
- winding resistance
- copper losses
- core losses at 50 kHz
- temperature rise
- physical size
- mounting requirements

Prefer a real commercially available
magnetic component where appropriate.

If no suitable standard component exists,
document the custom design requirements.

Include the actual component footprint,
physical dimensions and mechanical clearance
in PCB placement optimisation.

Prepare for future integration with
AIPE Magnetic Database.


==================================================
9. CAPACITOR DESIGN
==================================================

Use the existing DC-Link Capacitor Skill.

Evaluate:

Input DC-link capacitors:
- capacitance
- voltage rating
- RMS ripple current
- ESR / ESL
- film and ceramic capacitor combinations
- voltage derating
- thermal behaviour

Output capacitors:
- capacitance
- ripple-current rating
- output voltage ripple
- transient response
- ESR
- temperature and lifetime

Place high-frequency capacitors near
the switching power stage.

Minimise the associated high-frequency
commutation loop.

Do not select capacitors solely by capacitance.


==================================================
10. ELECTRICAL DESIGN VERIFICATION
==================================================

Complete and check the electrical design
before beginning PCB placement.

Verify:

- topology
- circuit connectivity
- switching operation
- driver voltage compatibility
- PWM operation
- dead time
- sensing interfaces
- current and voltage stresses
- startup behaviour
- fault protection
- inductor saturation margin
- capacitor ratings
- estimated losses
- thermal limits

Use appropriate analytical calculations
and circuit simulation where practical.

A plausible schematic is not sufficient.

Run KiCad ERC.

Correct all actionable errors before
advancing to PCB design.

If simulation cannot be executed, record
the limitation explicitly.

Do not substitute assumptions for
simulation results.


==================================================
11. PCB ARCHITECTURE SELECTION
==================================================

The agent should independently evaluate
different PCB architectures.

Option A:
Single integrated power and control PCB.

Option B:
Separate Power PCB and DSP Control PCB.

Option C:
Separate Power PCB, Gate Driver PCB and
DSP Control PCB, if justified.

Evaluate:

- total PCB area
- overall assembly volume
- layout complexity
- EMI
- grounding
- cooling
- manufacturing cost
- connector reliability
- modularity
- ease of testing
- future redesign

Do not automatically choose the same
architecture as V1.

The agent must explain the selected option.

Preferred layer counts:

Power PCB:
2–4 layers where technically feasible.

Control PCB:
Prefer 4 layers where practical.

6 layers are allowed if justified.

Do not add layers merely to simplify routing.

Do not sacrifice electrical integrity,
current capacity or isolation to reduce
layer count.


==================================================
12. POWER AND CONTROL PARTITIONING
==================================================

If separate boards are selected:

POWER BOARD:

- SiC MOSFETs
- gate drivers
- gate bias supplies
- DC-link capacitors
- output inductor
- output capacitors
- current sensing
- voltage sensing
- fast hardware protection
- thermal management

CONTROL BOARD:

- TMS320F28335
- DSP power supplies
- PWM logic
- ADC interfaces
- fault management
- communications
- JTAG/debug
- clock/reset
- auxiliary digital circuitry

Design an appropriate board-to-board
connection system.

Possible solutions:

- board-to-board connectors
- pin headers
- ribbon cables
- differential signal connectors

Choose based on actual signal integrity,
noise immunity, grounding and reliability.

Do not route high-current power through
ordinary signal connectors.

Keep high-speed gate-drive loops close
to the switching devices.

Do not separate the driver from the
power stage merely for visual symmetry.


==================================================
13. THERMAL AND MECHANICAL DESIGN
==================================================

Thermal design must influence placement.

Evaluate:

- MOSFET losses
- junction temperature
- package thermal resistance
- heatsink requirements
- electrical isolation from heatsink
- thermal interface materials
- inductor temperature
- capacitor temperature
- airflow
- cooling clearance
- mounting holes
- mechanical assembly

Reserve realistic heatsink and inductor
envelopes before routing.

Do not treat a component footprint as
the complete mechanical envelope.

Document the cooling assumptions and
thermal model limitations.


==================================================
14. EMI-AWARE PLACEMENT AND ROUTING
==================================================

The switching frequency is 50 kHz.

However, fast SiC switching transitions
introduce high-frequency parasitic effects.

Explicitly optimise:

- commutation-loop inductance
- gate-loop inductance
- switching-node copper geometry
- DC-link capacitor proximity
- gate-driver return path
- Kelvin source routing
- high-current copper paths
- analog signal return paths
- current-sense routing
- DSP/control separation
- common-mode coupling
- isolation boundaries

Apply relevant Engineering Skills
and reference-derived PCB rules.

Avoid unnecessary switching-node copper.

Keep sensitive sensing and feedback signals
away from noisy switching structures.

Use appropriate planes, zones, track geometry
and via structures.

Do not regard passing DRC as proof of
acceptable EMI performance.

==================================================

PCB compactness is an explicit design objective.

The agent should optimise:

- PCB area
- overall assembly volume
- placement density
- critical routing lengths
- copper utilisation
- power-loop geometry
- mechanical arrangement

Subject to:

- electrical correctness

- thermal requirements
- safety clearances
- creepage distances
- manufacturing constraints
- signal integrity
- EMI requirements

Do not blindly minimise the distance

Select the best justified design.

Document why the final placement was chosen.

21. SAFETY AND VALIDATION
==================================================

This is a 400 V DC, 5 kW non-isolated converter.

The 200 V output remains hazardous and
must not be treated as a safe low-voltage output.

The agent must consider:

- isolation of accessible control interfaces
- fault protection
- safe startup and shutdown
- input precharge/inrush
- DC-link discharge
- grounding and isolation boundaries
- safe test interfaces
- device short-circuit capability
- protective coordination
- thermal faults

Determine applicable electrical,
insulation and manufacturing standards
for the intended environment.

Do not invent generic clearance values
without a justified design basis.

This task ends at an engineering prototype
review package.

Do not fabricate, purchase components,
or energise the hardware.

The design must remain subject to
independent human engineering review.
