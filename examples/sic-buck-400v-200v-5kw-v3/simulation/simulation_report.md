# Executed V3 simulations

Run `python -m automation.sic_v3.simulation` using the existing SciPy environment and ngspice 46 DLL. `results/provenance.json` records source hashes. Native decks, simulator logs, CSV waveforms and SVG plots are retained. No vendor nonlinear SiC model was incorporated; there is no transistor-survival, extracted-parasitic, thermal or EMC validation claim.

## Models and observations

1. The averaged digital model includes current-dependent inductance, capacitor tolerance, sensor lags, 12-bit ADC quantisation, 1500-count PWM, PI saturation/anti-windup and one-period actuation delay. The event-aligned switched counterpart splits integration steps at every switching/deadtime boundary and includes channel resistance and body-diode drop. It does not include Coss, nonlinear capacitances or recovery physics.
2. A 200 ms current-controlled startup settles near 200 V. Switched peak current is about 28.0 A. A 12.5→25 A load step deviates about -14.0 V and settles to ±1% in 3.98 ms; the reverse step deviates +14.9 V and settles in 3.72 ms. The 360→440 V input step produces about 1.82 V instantaneous overshoot and 33.3 A instantaneous peak current. See the JSON for exact values. CSV output is sampled at 20 µs and cannot be used to infer switching ripple; extrema are tracked at all integration intervals, including PWM edges.
3. Exact-ZOH small-signal analysis includes two PI integrators, sensor filters and one sample delay. All 24 swept L/C/load/input corners have discrete poles inside the unit circle; minimum phase margin is approximately 67°. This proves stability only for the specified linear model. Saturation, parameter drift outside the sweep and hardware sampling jitter need separate validation. A 1 µs vs 2 µs integration comparison is recorded for the averaged load transient.
4. Independent native ngspice steady-state tests at 360/400/440 V produce approximately 5.07/5.72/6.26 A current ripple and 0.071/0.080/0.088 V output ripple. The source impedance is an explicit 10 µH/80 mΩ assumption. Native fixed-duty output differs slightly from the closed-loop model. The deck's 2 ms **open-loop** duty ramp overshoots to about 221 V and 46–47 A: it is retained as a failing counterexample and is not the V3 startup sequence. Its reported switch-model efficiency excludes switching/core/auxiliary losses and must not be quoted as converter efficiency.
5. DESAT RC simulations sweep capacitor tolerance and threshold. They include the off-state OC clamp, an approximate UF4007 model and the selected 680 pF network. Estimated detection plus maximum driver/soft-turnoff timing is roughly 1.35–1.53 µs in this model. No corresponding SiC short-circuit withstand guarantee exists in the retrieved data, so survival remains open.
6. Off-state Miller sensitivity sweeps 5/15/30 nH and 20/50/100 V/ns with a 0.5 Ω assumed active clamp. These are sensitivity inputs, not extracted nH. Native decks and logs are retained. Linearised Cgd and an ideal clamp cannot establish actual false-turn-on immunity.
7. `system_fault.json` and its waveform screen a bolted output short with minimum inductance, sensor under-reading and an assumed 8 µs total shutdown target. Gate inhibition leaves substantial current freewheeling through the low-side diode. This explicitly prevents treating PWM disable as galvanic isolation or immediate energy removal. Capacitor fault energy and fuses are outside this simplified trajectory.
