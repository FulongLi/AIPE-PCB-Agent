# Electrical design basis

Run `python -m automation.sic_v3.calculations`. Machine-readable results and the full operating-corner table are adjacent. These are analytical estimates; no measured efficiency, temperature, EMC or protection timing is available.

At 200 V/25 A the ideal duty range is 0.455–0.556 for 440–360 V. Inductor ripple is evaluated iteratively with incremental inductance at peak current, rather than the zero-current nameplate value: ΔI = Vout(1-D)/(L(Ipeak)fs). At 440 V, 25 A and AL -8%, L at the peak is approximately 348 µH and ripple is 6.27 A peak-to-peak. RMS uses I² + ΔI²/12. The capacitor-only plus ESR output-ripple bound is about 0.101 V; layout ESL and noise are excluded.

The input bank is 60 µF nominal; 54 µF is used for steady-state ripple. The output is 200 µF nominal, 180 µF for ripple/control and 220 µF for maximum stored energy/discharge. DC-link capacitor RMS follows the switching duty, including ripple, and must be checked with the real busbar distribution. Parallel ratings cannot be summed without accounting for current sharing and temperature.

MOSFET loss uses the 25°C guaranteed maximum resistance scaled by the typical 175°C ratio, with an additional 10% gate-voltage allowance. That composite is an estimate, not a guaranteed hot maximum. HS Eon+Eoff is scaled from the manufacturer's 800 V/39.2 A/175°C data and multiplied by 2.5 to screen the selected gate resistance and parasitics. Only the HS is hard switched in positive-current CCM; body-diode deadtime is explicitly counted. These approximations cannot predict VDS overshoot or qualify the switch.

The magnetic calculation uses the manufacturer's 60 µ bias/loss fits. Copper length is geometric, AWG10 resistance is temperature corrected, AC winding loss is screened at six times DC resistance for the ripple component, and core loss has a 1.3 waveform/DC-bias factor. These factors require a wound-sample loss and temperature test. The four cores alone weigh about 872 g; the PCB is not their mechanical support.

At the rated worst-input corner the present estimated total loss is about 107 W and efficiency about 97.9%. Copper loss is still a 2.5 W placeholder until the routed paths are screened; auxiliary consumption is a 12 W budget and contactor hold is another 1.7 W. Thermal assumptions are separately reviewed in `mechanical/thermal_interface.md`. Re-run final totals after layout: these values are not release results.

Gate-bias calculations solve the two independently regulated voltages and asymmetric midrail charge flow. Effective positive/negative reservoirs are assumed ≥2.2/10 µF; the raw total-rail MLCC bank must retain ≥10 µF at operating bias. The 1 kΩ / diode+180 Ω RDR network has source and sink margins >1 under the stated load/imbalance model. A 60 Ω internal resistance is a stress assumption, not a TI maximum. The independent sensing-bias load is 3×9.7 mA +1 mA, screened with 82 Ω RLIM.

Precharge uses 2.2 kΩ: initial power at 440 V and -1% R is 88.9 W; including permanent loading and worst C/R tolerance, the DC link comes within 8 V of raw input in about 0.715 s. Timeout is 1 s. The HS100 100 W rating requires a suitable heatsink; the no-heatsink continuous rating is only 30 W. Permanent bleed chains are 4×91 kΩ input and 4×43 kΩ output. At worst capacitance, +5% R and 440/225 V initial voltage, calculated decay to 60 V is about 53.7/52.5 s. Use actual measured capacitance and surface temperatures for qualification.

The ±38 A hardware window must allow the LEM temperature error of up to approximately ±2.4 A. With the **unverified** 8 µs system shutdown target and the 199 µH conservative minimum inductance, a worst continuously-on high-side screen gives about 58.1 A. This is not a short-circuit safe-operating-area limit. Output-capacitor discharge, fuse let-through, contactor interrupting duty and the source's prospective fault current remain release blockers.
